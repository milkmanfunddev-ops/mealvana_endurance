# Nutrition Guidance Content for Running Performance

## Scientific Rationale for Your Nutrition Targets

Your personalized nutrition plan is based on evidence from the International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) Position Stand and current sports nutrition research. Here's why each component matters for your running performance:

### Pre-Run Nutrition (1-4 Hours Before)

**Carbohydrates (1-4 g/kg body weight)**
Research shows that consuming carbohydrates 1-4 hours before running optimizes your muscle glycogen stores - your body's primary fuel source for endurance exercise. The ISSN recommends 1-4 g/kg of body weight, with timing affecting the amount: closer to exercise requires less (1 g/kg at 1 hour) while earlier timing allows more (up to 4 g/kg at 4 hours before).

**Protein (0.15-0.25 g/kg)**
While not essential pre-run, moderate protein intake can help maintain amino acid availability and may reduce muscle damage during your run, particularly for longer distances.

**Fat (<0.2 g/kg)**
Limiting fat intake before running is important as fat slows gastric emptying, which could cause GI distress during your run. Keep fat intake minimal in the pre-run window.

**Fluids (5-7 ml/kg)**
Pre-run hydration ensures you start in a euhydrated state. The ISSN recommends 5-7 ml/kg of body weight, consumed slowly over the hours before running to optimize absorption and minimize pre-run bathroom stops.

**Sodium (200-400mg)**
Sodium helps retain the fluids you consume and maintains proper electrolyte balance before you begin sweating during exercise.

### During-Run Nutrition

**Carbohydrates (30-60 g/hour for runs >2.5 hours)**
For runs lasting longer than 2.5 hours, the ISSN recommends 30-50 g/hour of carbohydrates to maintain blood glucose and delay fatigue. Your gut training level affects your tolerance - well-trained runners can often tolerate up to 60 g/hour. These carbohydrates help spare muscle glycogen and maintain performance, especially in the later stages of your run.

**Fluids (450-750 ml/hour)**
The ISSN recommends 450-750 ml/hour (approximately 15-25 fl oz) to offset sweat losses. Individual sweat rates vary greatly (0.4-1.8 L/hour), so this range represents typical needs. In hot conditions or at higher intensities, aim for the upper end of this range.

**Sodium (500-700 mg/L of fluid)**
Sodium is the primary electrolyte lost in sweat. The ISSN notes that typical sports drinks (containing ~20-25 mmol/L or 460-575 mg/L) may be insufficient for longer runs. Aim for 500-700 mg/L to maintain electrolyte balance and reduce the risk of hyponatremia, especially during runs exceeding 4 hours.

### Post-Run Recovery (Within 30 Minutes)

**Carbohydrates (1.0-1.2 g/kg body weight)**
Rapid glycogen resynthesis begins immediately post-exercise. The ISSN recommends 1.0-1.2 g/kg within the first hour to maximize glycogen replenishment, which is crucial if you're training again within 24 hours.

**Protein (0.25-0.3 g/kg)**
Post-run protein intake of 0.25-0.3 g/kg (typically 15-25g) optimizes muscle protein synthesis, aids recovery, and helps repair exercise-induced muscle damage. The ISSN notes this is particularly important after longer or more intense runs.

**Fluids (150% of body weight lost)**
To fully rehydrate, research shows you need to consume 150% of the fluid lost during exercise (measured by body weight change). This accounts for continued fluid losses through urine and respiration post-exercise.

**Sodium (500-1000mg)**
Sodium aids in fluid retention and helps restore electrolyte balance. Higher amounts may be needed after particularly sweaty runs or in hot conditions.

### Individual Variation

The ISSN emphasizes that individual responses to nutrition strategies vary considerably based on:
- Training status and gut adaptation
- Environmental conditions (heat, humidity, altitude)
- Running intensity and duration
- Personal tolerance and preferences
- Body composition and sweat rate

### Safety Considerations

These recommendations prioritize both performance and safety:
- Adequate carbohydrate intake prevents hypoglycemia (low blood sugar)
- Proper hydration prevents both dehydration and hyponatremia
- Electrolyte balance reduces cramping risk and maintains normal muscle function
- Appropriate timing minimizes GI distress

### Gut Training

The ISSN Position Stand emphasizes that the gut is trainable. Regular practice with your race nutrition strategy during training can:
- Increase carbohydrate absorption capacity
- Reduce GI distress
- Improve fluid tolerance
- Enhance overall comfort during running

Start conservatively and gradually increase intake during training to find your optimal nutrition strategy.

---

*These recommendations are based on the International Society of Sports Nutrition Position Stand on nutritional considerations for single-stage ultra-marathon training and racing (Tiller et al., 2019, Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition) and represent current evidence-based practice for endurance running nutrition.*
