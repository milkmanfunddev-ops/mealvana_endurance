Creating a New iOS App on App Store Connect
Now that you’ve registered your App ID, it’s time to create a new app on App Store Connect and prepare it for review.

This is a long process and you’ll need to provide a lot of information.

To guide you through all the required steps, I created these four lessons:

Metadata, screenshots, and app review details (this lesson)
App information and age ratings
App privacy and data collection practices
Pricing and availability
Let’s get started!

If your app uses certain features (e.g., In-App Purchases), additional details may be required.

Creating a New App
Go to App Store Connect > Apps, then add a new app:

Create a New App
Fill in the required details:

Create a New App
These include:

Platforms: Ensure iOS is selected.
Name: The app name displayed to users on the App Store.
Primary Language: The default language for your app’s metadata.
Bundle ID: Select the App ID you registered in the previous lesson.
SKU: A unique ID for your app (not visible to users).
User Access: Decide which App Store Connect users can manage the app.
Preparing for Review
After clicking Create, you’ll land on the app’s overview page:

App Overview
Here, you’ll enter all the required app details before submitting it for review. If any information is missing, you’ll see an error when you click Add for Review:

Unable to Add for Review
Now, let’s go through all the required steps. 👇

Preview and Screenshots
Screenshots are one of the most important elements of your App Store listing. They are often the first thing users see, so make sure they sell the benefits of using your app (and not merely show your app’s features).

Creating professional screenshots is very important, but it’s also a very time-consuming process. That’s why I created a whole module about automated screenshot generation. But for now, let’s stay on track.

You can upload screenshots for different form factors by clicking View All Sizes in Media Manager. If you’re short on time, one set of screenshots is enough for now, but they must match the required dimensions. Here’s an example:

Previews and Screenshots
Make sure you upload screenshots with the correct size, otherwise you’ll get an upload error.

App Metadata
After adding the screenshots, scroll down and fill the remaining fields, like in this example:

Description, keywords, Support URL, Marketing URL
Note for apps offering auto-renewable subscriptions
If your app offers auto-renewable subscriptions, you’ll need to include a functional link to the Privacy Policy and Terms of Use (EULA):

In your app’s paywall screen
In the App Store description
If you don’t do this, Apple will reject your submission.

To address this, add your custom EULA or the standard EULA from Apple in your App Store description. Example:

Terms of Use: https://www.apple.com/legal/internet-services/itunes/dev/stdeula/
App Review Information
Scroll past the Build section (we’ll revisit this later), and you’ll land on App Review Information, where you can provide additional details for the App Store Review Team. This includes:

Contact Information: If the reviewers need to reach out to you for clarification.
Sign-in Information: If your app requires login, provide a test account for the reviewers.
Description, keywords, Support URL, Marketing URL
Sign-In Information
If users must sign in to use your app, create a test account for the Apple review team and provide the credentials here. Otherwise, leave the Sign-in required option unchecked.
