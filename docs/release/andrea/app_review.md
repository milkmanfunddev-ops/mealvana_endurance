The App Review Process
Once you’ve submitted your app to App Store Connect, it enters the App Review Process, where Apple’s review team evaluates your app to ensure it complies with their App Store Review Guidelines. This is a crucial step, as any issues found during the review can lead to a rejection, delaying your app’s release.

Why the App Store Review Guidelines Matter
The App Store Review Guidelines outline all of Apple’s requirements for apps submitted to the App Store. These guidelines cover everything from functionality and design to privacy and security. Apple takes these guidelines seriously, and failing to comply can result in your app being rejected—delaying your release.

Some of the most common reasons for rejection include non-compliance with privacy policies, improper use of Apple trademarks, crashes or bugs, and misleading content. By familiarizing yourself with the guidelines, you can avoid these issues and ensure a smoother review process.

While the guidelines are extensive and can feel overwhelming at first, every developer who is serious about publishing iOS apps should read them at least once. Understanding them thoroughly will help you avoid common mistakes and undesired rejections, saving you time and frustration.

The best place to start is this page about App Review, which contains important information about preparing and submitting your app for review, along with guidance about avoiding common issues.

Tip to Avoid Rejection
Read the section about avoiding common issues and take the time to review Apple’s guidelines before submitting your app. A good understanding of the rules means fewer surprises and a smoother path to approval!

Stages of the App Review Process
1. Prepare for Submission
This is the stage where you fill all the necessary information and upload your app, as described in the previous lesson. If everything is in order, clicking on Add for Review will take you to the next stage.

Key actions:

Ensure all metadata, privacy details, and compliance information are complete.
Select the app build.
Click Add for Review.
2. Ready for Review
At this stage, all the pre-review checks have been made, and you can submit the app for review.

Key actions:

Click Submit to App Review.
Fun Fact
More than once, I’ve been wondering why the app review was taking too long, only to realize I forgot to click on Submit to App Review. Don’t make the same mistake. 😅

3. Waiting for Review
Once you submit your app, it enters the review queue. Apple’s review team will start reviewing your app based on their guidelines. This is often the longest part of the process, as it can take anywhere from a few hours to several days.

Key actions:

Wait for the app to move to the next stage.
During this time, Apple may reach out for additional information, so keep an eye on your email.
4. In Review
At this stage, Apple’s review team is actively reviewing your app. They will check for things like adherence to App Review Guidelines, functionality, performance, privacy compliance, and more. If the app passes all checks, it will move to the next stage.

Key actions:

No action is required unless Apple contacts you for clarification or requests changes.
The app review team may test your app on devices and check compliance with privacy policies and other guidelines.
5. Pending Developer Release (Optional)
If you’ve chosen manual release when setting up your app’s availability, once your app passes review, it will enter the Pending Developer Release state. This means the app is approved but won’t be available on the App Store until you manually release it.

Key actions:

Review everything and ensure the app is ready for the public.
Once you’re ready, click Release This Version to make your app available on the App Store.
6. Ready for Distribution
Once your app is either automatically or manually released, it enters the Ready for Distribution stage. At this point, the app is live on the App Store, and users can download or purchase it.

Key actions:

Monitor the app’s performance, ratings, and reviews.
Start tracking app analytics and user feedback.
Respond to any issues that arise post-launch (e.g., bug fixes, updates).
Sometimes, the app may briefly enter a Processing for Distribution stage before becoming fully available. This normally lasts less than one hour.

The steps above represent the happy path. But if something goes wrong, the app may enter one of the stages below.

Optional Stages
Rejected: If Apple finds issues with your app, they may reject it. You’ll receive a rejection notification with a reason (or multiple reasons) and suggested fixes. At this point, you’ll need to address the issues, submit a new build, and re-enter the review process.
Metadata Rejected: This happens if there’s an issue with the app’s metadata (e.g., app description, screenshots). The app build itself is fine, but you’ll need to update the metadata before the app can proceed through the review process.
Developer Rejected: You can always choose to reject your own app if you need to make changes before it’s reviewed. This withdraws the app from the review queue.

What to Do If Your App Is Rejected
If your app is rejected, you’ll receive an email that looks like this:

We noticed an issue with your submission
This email will include the reasons for rejection, along with links to the relevant sections of the App Store Review Guidelines.

Here’s what you should do if your app is rejected:

Review the Reasons for Rejection: Carefully read through the rejection reasons and use the provided links to understand which guidelines were violated.
Fix the Issues: Make the necessary changes in your app to address the rejection reasons. This might involve fixing bugs, updating your metadata, or adjusting your app’s functionality.
Resubmit Your App: Upload a new build, update the metadata if needed, and submit your app for review again.
If you need clarification or don’t understand the rejection reasons, you can reach out to the review team. Head to the Contacting Us section, where you can learn how to correspond with the App Review team.

If you believe your app was unfairly rejected, you can also Submit an Appeal to App Review.
