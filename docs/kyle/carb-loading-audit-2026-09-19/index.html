Carb loading, start to dead end

I built an Ironman 70.3 Augusta on 28 September, started the 3-Day Classic protocol, and used the loading page as an athlete would. The protocol maths is sound. Everything around it — how you get back in, what it counts toward, how you tap 544 grams into it — is not.

The route I took

Five taps in, and the door locks behind you

  1. 01
    Events → New Event → Triathlon → Half Ironman / 70.3
    Name, date 28 Sep, create. Clean. The autocomplete misfired — see finding 12.
  2. 02
    Event Details → Create Carb Loading Plan
    One of three buttons under “Nutrition Planning”. Nothing on the page says what a carb loading plan is or when it would start.
  3. 03
    Choose Carb Loading Protocol → Select 3-Day Protocol
    Two cards, 3-Day Classic recommended. This screen is a different design language from the rest of the app.
  4. 04
    Lands directly on Carb Loading Day 1 — Friday, Sep 25
    No plan overview, no “here are your three days”. Straight into one day’s logging surface.
  5. 05
    Tap Done → dropped to the Events list. The page is now gone.
    Day 1 is never reachable again. Day 2 and Day 3 were never reachable at all.

Credit where it’s due

What already works

  • The protocol science Real citations on the cards — Sherman, Burke, Bergström & Hultman, Fairchild. Rates and phases match the literature.
  • Body-weight personalisation 68 kg × 8 g·kg⁻¹ = 544 g, computed correctly and surfaced in Edit Target with the athlete’s own weight.
  • Inline quantity steppers Tap a chip and it becomes a −/+ pill in place. Fast, no dialog, no page change. This is the best interaction in the feature.
  • Overload warning Cross the day target and an orange “OVERLOADED BY 32G” banner appears under the total. The right idea, at the wrong altitude — see finding 6.
  • Meal-aware food lists Breakfast offers bagels and oats, lunch offers pasta and potatoes. Only three distinct lists underneath, but the intent is right.
  • Escape hatches Reset Progress is confirmed before it fires. Edit Target offers both g·kg⁻¹ and total grams. Custom foods and barcode scan are both wired in.
12 findings, worst first

Pain points

Blocker

1 · The page is a one-shot

Carb Loading Day 1 is pushed with pushReplacement the instant the plan is created, and that is the only navigation to it in the whole app. Afterwards the button relabels to Edit Carb Loading Plan, which re-opens the protocol chooser; picking a protocol calls the update branch, shows a snackbar, and leaves you on Event Details. Days 2 and 3 have no route at any point.

lib/features/events/presentation/widgets/event_action_buttons_card.dart:186–265

Evidence · Edit → snackbar dead end, Done → Events list

Blocker

2 · Logged carbs never reach the rest of the app

The day page totals its meals correctly on screen, but carb_loading_days.logged_carbs_grams is never written — it stayed 0 with 179 g logged. That column is what the coach portal renders as the athlete’s progress, what the old dashboard card drew its bar from, and what syncs to Supabase. A coach watching an athlete carb load sees 0/544 g forever.

Read at portal_athlete_detail_panel.dart:488 and carb_loading_day_card.dart:19; written by nobody. The update path exists (carb_loading_repository.dart:503) and has no caller.

Blocker

3 · Carb loading is a parallel universe

Nothing outside lib/features/carb_loading reads the meals table. With 179 g logged against Friday 25 September, the Timeline for that day shows +0 kcal, “on track”, and an empty feed; the calendar puts no dot on 25, 26 or 27, nor on race day. An athlete mid-load has to log every bagel twice or accept that one of the two surfaces is lying.

Evidence · Timeline, Fri 25 Sep, September calendar

Blocker

4 · Re-picking a protocol silently destroys logged food

Choosing a protocol on an existing plan regenerates the day rows with fresh IDs and drops their meals. No warning, no confirmation — just “Updated to 3-day carb loading plan!”. Since the protocol chooser is the only thing “Edit Carb Loading Plan” opens, the one route back into the feature is also the one that wipes it.

Major

5 · Sixty chips, one per line, five and a half screens

Day 1 opens with about 60 tappable food chips stacked one per row before anything is logged — roughly 5½ phone screens of scrolling at 0 g. Lunch and dinner are served the identical 7-item list; the three snack slots are served the identical 12-item list. The morning snack alone — a 54 g target — fills a whole screen with twelve chips.

Evidence · Day 1 at rest, Morning snack, 12 chips for 54 g

Major

6 · You can’t see what you’ve eaten

Selected chips stay in place among the unselected ones rather than collecting anywhere, so “what have I actually had today” means scanning 60 rows for teal. And the per-meal badge never warns: eight bagels in a 136 g breakfast reads 576/136g in a calm teal pill. Only the day total turns orange.

Evidence · Partially logged day, 576/136g still teal

Major

7 · Chip order reshuffles between refreshes

Breakfast opened as Cereal, Banana, Bagel, Toast, Pancake, Waffle… and came back from the food picker as Waffle, Toast, Smoothie, Pancake, Orange juice, Oats, Cereal, Banana, Bagel. Nothing is pinned, nothing is sorted. Three of eight scripted taps landed on the wrong food because the list moved underneath them — a real athlete will do the same with their thumb.

Major

8 · Carbs only, for a race where fluid and sodium are the protocol

Every surface counts grams of carbohydrate and nothing else — no calories, protein, fat, fibre, fluid or sodium, and daily_calorie_target on the plan row is empty. Eating 680 g of carbohydrate on a Sunday is also ~2,700 kcal and a large fluid and sodium job; the page never mentions the last part. Fibre matters too, and the day before a 70.3 it matters in the other direction.

Major

9 · Plan and log are the same act

Adding a chip is logging it as consumed — there is no way to lay out tomorrow’s 544 g in advance and then tick it off. For a protocol whose entire point is preparing three days ahead, the page only supports the present tense. “Mark Complete” also surfaces the moment the day total crosses target even when five of six meals are empty.

Major

10 · The protocol screen is off-brand, and some of it is invisible

The phase cards render as white Material cards with cream titles — “3–2 days out” and “1 day out” are unreadable. The Show / Hide Details toggle is near-invisible on the dark ground; I tapped it blind. Around them: pale-blue Material chips, a Material-green RECOMMENDED badge, emoji section markers, and a title that truncates to “Choose Carb Loading Pr…”. The file paints with Color(0xFF4CAF50) and Colors.grey[600] directly, against the one-token-registry rule.

Major

11 · The food picker wastes four fifths of the screen

Rows carry enormous vertical dead space — four items per screen, with stark white circular fork-and-knife placeholders that read as broken images. Tapping a food replaces the entire list with a quantity editor in place, with no way back to the list and the search box cleared. The quantity is a bare “1.0” with no unit, and only carbohydrate is shown.

Evidence · Add Food to Breakfast, Quantity editor

Smaller things

12 · Seven that each cost a few seconds

Guidance contradicts itself — the protocol card says 7–9 g·kg⁻¹ for days 3–2 out; Edit Target’s help text says “8-12g/kg per day during carb loading”. “Best For” ignores the event you came from — reached from a 70.3, the 3-Day Classic still lists Marathon, Half Marathon, Long distance races, with no triathlon anywhere. Event search relevance — typing the exact name “Ironman 70.3 Augusta” returned Cozumel and Valdivia; typing “Augusta 70.3 (probe)” found the real IRONMAN 70.3 Augusta (27 Sep 2026). The EVENT INFORMATION card renders empty. The Create Custom Food FAB clips the “Search Open Food Facts” button beneath it. The custom food form asks for both “Food Name” and “Display Name (with serving)”, and offers “Snacks” beside Morning, Afternoon and Evening Snack. Reset Progress fires without a confirmation toast — the totals just drop to zero.
