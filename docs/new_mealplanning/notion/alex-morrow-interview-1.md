## Content

Coaches who aren't licensed nutritionists face a scope-of-practice wall that limits nutrition coaching to anecdotal guidance and manufacturer label recommendations — Alex estimates a 75% success rate getting athletes to fuel properly, with the remaining 25% "knowing better" but refusing to change behavior in practice. His current approach is entirely reactive: nutrition only gets attention when performance drops, using the scientific method of removing variables rather than proactive monitoring. He sees massive value in a tool that sits in his coaching toolkit without requiring him to "be a dietitian" — specifically one that integrates with training platforms like Final Surge to show causation and correlation between fueling and workout performance. He explicitly validates the actionable-meals approach over macro percentages, citing his own overwhelm working with a dietitian who gave him numbers without telling him what to eat. Different marketing is needed for runners (performance plateau angle, coach-first channel) vs. triathletes (data-loving, easier sell).

## Key Themes

### "I Can Only Guide You" — The 75% Success Rate Ceiling on Nutrition Behavior Change
Alex estimates about 75% of his athletes eventually improve their fueling when coached incrementally. The other 25% are a dead end: *"he understands what I'm saying. But in practice, he just doesn't do it."* [↗ alexmorrow](https://app.notion.com/p/337e3fdb754c8085947fd8578da79c13#1f2f74394135434f9ed9b641d99b13f4) These athletes know the science but won't act on it. Alex has matured past frustration — *"you're an adult, and I can only give… I can only guide you"* [↗ alexmorrow](https://app.notion.com/p/337e3fdb754c8085947fd8578da79c13#b104bfd54bff46dfac00212e8663946a) — but he uses poor race performance as leverage: *"we're not going to talk about your running if you're not going to talk about the nutrition."* [↗ alexmorrow](https://app.notion.com/p/337e3fdb754c8085947fd8578da79c13#3122857b86b14a0aab46023a950176c4) A passive accountability tool could close this gap without requiring the coach to play enforcer.

### "I'm Not a Dietitian" — Scope-of-Practice Anxiety as the Primary Barrier to Nutrition Coaching
This was the strongest and most repeated theme. Alex explicitly limits himself because of scope of practice: *"I am a running coach, I'm not a dietitian, I'm not a nutritionist, so we try to speak anecdotally."* [↗ alexmorrow](https://app.notion.com/p/337e3fdb754c8085947fd8578da79c13#5425c0974199488bab4b188d9b019237) He references state licensure variability and personal liability — *"if I start designing meal plans for you, if something bad happens, I'm liable for that."* [↗ alexmorrow](https://app.notion.com/p/337e3fdb754c8085947fd8578da79c13#93f1503042c447a0990ccde55061c427) As one of 8 RRCA coaching instructors in the country, he teaches new coaches this same principle: *"you have to stay in your lane."* [↗ alexmorrow](https://app.notion.com/p/337e3fdb754c8085947fd8578da79c13#df583f2410f6462bb993d347c1a88098) This is the exact gap Mealvana Endurance fills — giving coaches a tool backed by science and research so the coach doesn't have to be the nutrition expert.

### The Manufacturer's Label as a Proxy for Expertise — Outsourcing Nutrition Authority to Product Packaging
Because Alex won't prescribe specific nutrition, he defers to manufacturer recommendations as a stand-in for expertise: *"I'm not saying take it every 45 minutes, the manufacturer is saying take it every 45 minutes."* [↗ alexmorrow](https://app.notion.com/p/337e3fdb754c8085947fd8578da79c13#df3e5c628144482885ad529935d48322) He uses the gel package label as the "expert" voice. This workaround reveals how desperately coaches need a credible intermediary — they want to give nutrition guidance but need something authoritative between them and the athlete. Single mention of this specific workaround — low confidence as a standalone pattern, but strongly reinforces the scope-of-practice theme.

### The Incremental Trust-Building Protocol — "Could You Try Half a Banana?"
Alex describes a gradual, trust-based ramp-up for both pre-run and mid-run fueling. He starts with *"half a banana before you go run"* [↗ alexmorrow](https://app.notion.com/p/337e3fdb754c8085947fd8578da79c13#5fd889c308404d6cbb8f0177314b7e86) and increases run by run: *"on the next run, I'm going to say, let's up it a little bit more."* [↗ alexmorrow](https://app.notion.com/p/337e3fdb754c8085947fd8578da79c13#962b8ddb99ab4e599220004bfeb79847) He tailors alternatives when preferences conflict (*"I don't like bananas — great, how about a rice cake with a little bit of nut butter"* [↗ alexmorrow](https://app.notion.com/p/337e3fdb754c8085947fd8578da79c13#5fd889c308404d6cbb8f0177314b7e86)). This mirrors the progressive gut adaptation protocol that other coaches (Bolton, Burl) describe, but Alex's version is entirely informal and untracked. A tool that supports progressive fueling ramps would formalize what good coaches already do intuitively.

### "Not Broken, Don't Fix It" — Nutrition Is Invisible Until Performance Crashes
Alex only addresses nutrition when an athlete starts missing workouts or paces. If Sean is *"killing everything,"* he presumes her nutrition is dialed in. When performance drops, he applies the *"scientific method of removing variables"* — sleep, stress, nutrition. This reactive model means fueling problems compound silently until a bad workout or race exposes them. Proactive nutrition visibility (even just a coach dashboard showing athlete fueling consistency) could surface issues before they become performance crises.

### Athletes Don't Communicate Because the Friction Is Too High, Not Because They Don't Care
Alex identifies athlete communication as his biggest coaching frustration — but he credits platforms like Final Surge with dramatically improving it: *"having the software made it so much easier, because it's just clicking a button as opposed to calling me or texting me."* [↗ alexmorrow](https://app.notion.com/p/337e3fdb754c8085947fd8578da79c13#bed0e4095ccc4b8399b3804bdcadff40) The insight: athletes aren't withholding information out of laziness or secrecy. They need frictionless reporting mechanisms. Nutrition logging fails for the same reason pre-platform workout reporting failed — the ask is too heavy. A one-tap fueling log in the training ecosystem could replicate what Final Surge did for workout communication.

### The Training Platform Integration Vision — "Causation and Correlation"
Alex unprompted articulated the exact product vision: a nutrition app synced with Final Surge or TrainingPeaks so coaches can see fueling data alongside workout results. *"If you could have your app, which syncs with those platforms, now we can actually see causation and correlation."* [↗ alexmorrow](https://app.notion.com/p/337e3fdb754c8085947fd8578da79c13#f2061d11e943482389f6f0a596ce01bc) He believes *"there's no one doing that currently"* [↗ alexmorrow](https://app.notion.com/p/337e3fdb754c8085947fd8578da79c13#2534129104e445629610fd6fad2e9ec1) and calls it *"super beneficial."* He also offered to personally introduce the team to Tim Surface (Final Surge CEO), having a strong existing relationship with him. This validates the integration-first strategy and provides a warm path to a key partnership.

### Runners vs. Triathletes Require Different Marketing Messaging — Data Appetite Varies by Sport
Alex drew a clear distinction: triathletes *"like the data, they like all the number crunch, they like all the minutiae of the training. I don't find that as much with runners."* [↗ alexmorrow](https://app.notion.com/p/337e3fdb754c8085947fd8578da79c13#a05b8ec7508a4eddb36aab5e04f0bde2) He recommended different channels: for runners, approach through coaches and frame around performance plateaus (*"are you trying to improve your performance? Have you had a plateau?"* [↗ alexmorrow](https://app.notion.com/p/337e3fdb754c8085947fd8578da79c13#f93e77a85a6b4a93819f60f6c0201b7d)). Triathletes and ultra runners are *"an easy sell"* because they already understand fueling is essential for 12-24 hour events. He also advised test-balloon marketing — launch messaging six ways, analyze traction in 2-3 weeks, and double down on what works.

### The Dietitian Information Overload Problem — Actionability Beats Data Every Time
Alex's own experience with a dietitian (Dr. Van Horn) mirrors the Fuelin problem that Ryan Bolton reported: *"she gave me so much information that it was overwhelming. I didn't know what to do with it."* [↗ alexmorrow](https://app.notion.com/p/337e3fdb754c8085947fd8578da79c13#f359a710ea1745518540825349bc567f) He needed multiple follow-up conversations to translate macros into actual behavior. When Xuan described Mealvana's grocery-list and meal-level approach, Alex responded: *"the fact that you make it actionable will make you successful, for sure."* [↗ alexmorrow](https://app.notion.com/p/337e3fdb754c8085947fd8578da79c13#3e3887a5c657432e99a2f84753506e23) This directly validates the product's core differentiator against percentage-based competitors.

## Product/Feature Requests
- Integration with Final Surge, TrainingPeaks, and possibly VDOT — ability to correlate nutrition with workout performance data
- Coach-facing dashboard showing prescribed nutrition plan vs. actual athlete intake (*"here's what actually happened"*)
- Electrolyte/sodium replacement recommendations based on individual sweat composition (referenced Bionic Sensor sweat rate tests)
- Frictionless athlete reporting — one-tap or automatic, not manual food logging
