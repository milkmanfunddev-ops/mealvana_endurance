## 💡 Synthesized Insights

**Primary Pain Point:**
Scope-of-practice anxiety is the dominant barrier — Alex won't build meal plans or give specific nutrition prescriptions because he's not a licensed dietitian/nutritionist. He defers to manufacturer labels, book recommendations, and dietitian referrals as workarounds. He needs a credible, science-backed tool that lets him address athlete nutrition without crossing professional boundaries or creating liability.

**Secondary Pain Points:**
- ~25% of athletes refuse to fuel properly despite logically understanding the importance — behavior change is the bottleneck, not education
- Nutrition is only addressed reactively when performance drops; no proactive monitoring or visibility exists
- No tool correlates nutrition data with training platform workout data — coaches can't see the relationship between fueling and performance
- Athletes' lack of nutrition communication is a friction problem (too many steps to report), not a motivation problem
- App carb recommendations (60-90g/hour) conflict with manufacturer gel dosing guidelines, creating liability exposure — Alex's attorney spouse heightens this concern
- Checking a separate nutrition portal outside Final Surge adds workflow friction coaches won't tolerate except reactively
- Manual sync buttons signal product immaturity — auto-sync is the adoption baseline, not a feature
- "Coach Mode" naming implies coaches should actively build nutrition plans, triggering scope-of-practice anxiety and perceived workload addition

**Product Implications:**
- Alex explicitly described Mealvana's value proposition: a tool that sits in his coaching toolkit without requiring him to "be a dietitian" — strong product-market fit signal
- Integration with Final Surge is the priority — Alex has a direct relationship with CEO Tim Surface and offered to make introductions
- Actionable meals/grocery lists validated as the key differentiator over macro-percentage tools (Fuelin) — Alex's own dietitian experience confirms this gap
- Coach-facing dashboard should default to read-only summary view (carbs/hour, sodium, nutrition-performance correlation) — not plan-building tools
- His 8-coach, ~150-runner team represents a strong pilot group with real scale; workshop confirmed April 16th targeting ~50 committed testers
- RRCA conference is a high-leverage marketing venue — Alex can connect team to RRCA CEO Jean Kanak
- Manufacturer liability gap is a systemic product risk: all gel-heavy fueling plans need disclaimers referencing manufacturer dosing, palate fatigue, and varied carb sources
- "Coach Mode" name triggers scope-of-practice anxiety — rename to position as workload-reduction tool, not additional responsibility
- Auto-sync is a hygiene factor, not a differentiator — manual sync buttons undermine credibility
