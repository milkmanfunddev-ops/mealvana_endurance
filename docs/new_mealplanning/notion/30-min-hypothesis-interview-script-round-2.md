## Purpose
A 30-minute, **prototype-free** interview that tests five hypotheses about how endurance athletes plan, shop, and decide what to eat. Replaces the prototype-driven format used in May 2026 round 1.

## Hypotheses tested
- **H-1:** Users want to be told what to eat (assuming the program is smart).
- **H-2:** Users want to stick to a macro-based plan.
- **H-3 (revised):** A conversational interface, when delivered at concierge quality, increases plan adherence vs. a button-driven prescription.
- **H-4:** Users plan (or are willing to plan) meals before grocery shopping.
- **H-5:** Users have a mix of recipe-driven and ingredient-driven meal choices, with most preferring ingredient-driven.

## Pre-interview screener (text, ~24 hrs before)
Two questions. Both must pass before booking the interview.
1. *"When did you last write or save a shopping list before going to the store?"* — If "I don't really do that," defer to a separate study about non-planners.
2. *"Do you currently work with a coach for running or triathlon? If yes, who?"* — A yes is preferred for this round.

## Script

**Total target: 30 minutes. Time targets are real.** If you're behind by minute 15, cut Section 5 and go straight to commitment.

### Section 0 — Setup (2 min)
> Thanks for making time. We're testing some ideas — not a product, not a design — about how endurance athletes plan meals. No prototype today, just a conversation. We're going to talk about how you actually eat and shop right now, then I'll describe some concepts and ask what you think. There are no right or wrong answers. Recording, OK with you?

### Section 1 — Behavioral baseline (6 min)
*Tests H-4 and H-5 implicitly. Pure observation — do not introduce any concepts yet.*
- "Walk me through your last grocery trip. When did you make the list, and how did you decide what was on it?"
- "What did you eat for dinner this past Tuesday?"
- "What about the Tuesday before?"
- "When you cook, do you generally look up a recipe or improvise from what's in the kitchen? Tell me about last weekend."
- "Where do your saved recipes live — Pinterest, screenshots, app? Show me one you've actually made more than once."

If they say "I don't really plan" or "no list" — **note it, don't redirect**. That answers H-4.

### Section 2 — H-1: Prescription (4 min)
- "Tell me about the last time someone else decided what you'd eat — a partner, a meal kit, a coach's recommendation. How did it feel?"
- "When you opened your last [Cook Unity / Home Chef / EveryPlate] box, did you follow the recipe exactly or change it? What did you change?"

Then introduce the concept **verbally — no screens, no paper**:
- "Imagine an assistant that knows your training, your preferences, your schedule. Each Sunday it tells you exactly what you'll eat for the week — 21 meals — and gives you a shopping list. You can swap any meal you don't like. First reaction?"
- "Now imagine the same assistant gives you three different weekly plans to pick from instead. Which feels better?"

The contrast between prescription-with-override and curated-choice is the data. **Don't lead them toward either.**

### Section 3 — H-2: Macros (4 min)
- "When you ate dinner last night, do you know roughly how many grams of protein you got? Did you check?"
- "What do you look at on a food label?"
- "Have you ever tracked macros for more than a week at a time? Tell me about that."

### Section 4 — H-3: Conversational vs. button-driven (6 min)
**This is the most important hypothesis section. Protect the time.**

Read both vignettes aloud. No screens. ~90 seconds per vignette, then 3 minutes on the comparison.

> **Vignette A.** It's Sunday morning. You open the app. You see your training load this week, the weather, peak produce. You tap one button — "Plan Week." Twenty-one meals appear, organized into a shopping list. If you don't like a meal, three alternatives appear next to it; tap one to swap. The whole thing takes 90 seconds.
- "How does that feel?"

> **Vignette B.** Sunday morning. You open the app. You get a message from your assistant: "Hey [name] — looking at your training and the heat wave through Wednesday, want to plan around no-cook dinners? Tell me your protein for Sunday's long-run carb load." You reply "steak." It proposes a meal — accept or swap. Conversation continues until the plan is full. Takes about 8 minutes but feels like a chat with a knowledgeable friend.
- "How does that feel?"
- "Which Sunday would you rather have? Walk me through why."
- **Killer follow-up:** "Is your answer about the experience itself, or about how long it takes? If both took the same time, would your answer change?"

### Section 5 — H-5: Recipe vs. ingredient concept probe (3 min)
*Cut this section if behind schedule. Section 1 already collects most of the H-5 data.*

Show on paper:

> **Plan A** — Mediterranean Salmon Bowl with Quinoa. 5-step recipe. Full ingredient list. Photo.

[image: Plan A stimulus — Mediterranean Salmon Bowl with Quinoa, recipe card with photo]

> **Plan B** — This week's protein: 4 servings of cooked salmon. Carb: 5 cups of cooked quinoa. Veggies: roasted zucchini, tomatoes, peppers. Sauce: lemon-tahini. Assemble dinners from these.

[image: Plan B stimulus — component/ingredient-driven card]

- "Which would you rather follow this week?"
- **Disentangling follow-up:** "Is that what you'd *prefer*, or what's *realistic for you* this week?"

### Section 6 — Commitment and close (3 min)
**Non-negotiable. Always make time for this.**
- "Of everything we just talked about, what one thing would change your weekly meal routine the most if it existed?"
- "We're piloting a service where every Sunday you get a personalized meal plan and shopping list for the week. Free for the first month, no obligation after. Want to sign up?" → **Capture email immediately if yes.**
- "Anything I should have asked that I didn't?"

## Moderator rules
Print these and put them on the desk before the interview.
1. **After the participant speaks, count to three before responding.** Most leading happens in the immediate verbal reaction. Silence pulls more out.
2. **Never explain what something does until they've explored or guessed on their own.**
3. **Don't introduce concepts they haven't raised.** No volunteering "high carb day," "fresh produce," "macros," "training load" unless they say it first.
4. **When they say something you like, don't reinforce it.** Replace "yeah, that's a great point" with "tell me more about that." Positive reinforcement biases the rest of the session.
5. **When they contradict an earlier statement, name it neutrally.** "Earlier you said X, just now you said Y — help me understand." Don't paper over inconsistencies; they're often the most valuable signal.
6. **Don't ask "would you...?" Ask "when did you last...?"** Stated intent is near-worthless. Past behavior is gold.
7. **End each section with: "Anything else on this I should have asked?"**

### Real-time decisions
- If they're talking and it's gold, let them talk. Cut a later section, not the current insight.
- If they're talking and it's off-topic, redirect after one sentence, not three.
- **Section 4 is the most important. Protect it.** If you're behind, cut Section 5, not Section 4.
- **Section 6 commitment ask is non-negotiable.** That's the data that survives the interview.

---

## Post-interview
- Send the first Sunday plan to email-capture participants 1 week and 4 weeks later. **Don't ask "would you still like to be in the pilot?"** Just send and measure open and cook follow-through. Open rate is the cleanest intent signal.
- Note any cross-cutting insight that should be probed in subsequent interviews — when 3+ participants surface the same pattern, it's the round's headline finding.
