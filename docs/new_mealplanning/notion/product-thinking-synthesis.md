- **Feb 2026:** "Meal Planning Algorithm" doc — the concrete pre-LLM-rewrite scoring pipeline, reviewed and criticized in comments by Lee and others (point-value disputes, request for A/B validation).

- **May 8, 2026:** Major design-review meeting — three competing prototypes presented (Ray's "Daily Planning with Emotional Support," Xuan's "Weekly Planning with Rich UI"/chat-forward, Lee's "Hybrid UI-Assisted"/"Jade"). - **Aug 2026:** Meal planning still an open, actively-assigned task ("Work on the meal-planning design Lee asked for," due 2026-08-25, owner Xuan) — confirms the feature was still not shipped as of the most recent artifacts in this archive.

## 2. Decisions made

- **Positioning: NOT a generic meal planner.** "Bridge the gap between workout fueling and the rest of their life" / "We help you eat right even when you're not training" — the callout at the top of the core user-research synthesis doc, repeated as the guiding product goal throughout later docs.
- **Model rejected: MFP/Fueling-style macro-first tracking.** Explicit early competitive framing: MAVR (pure LLM-generated recipes, no DB) and "Fueling" (macro-matched but often impractical recipes) were both judged as gaps to win against on *execution*, not just personalization.
- **Model chosen: Hungry Root-style minimal-effort, batch-cook, partially-prepped-food planning** — explicitly named as the design north star in the 2026-04-03 meeting, over complex from-scratch recipe planning.
- **Initial scope: single athletes without families first**; family/household complexity deferred as a v2+ problem, even though family constraints were independently identified as a major real-world barrier in user research.
- **Race-day fueling and meal planning are architecturally separate, and must stay that way.** The existing deterministic before/during/after fueling module is NOT to be routed through the LLM — explicit rationale: risk of hallucinated/protocol-ignorant advice, and the deterministic system already works. `meal_planning_budget = daily_total_target − performance_nutrition_allocated` is the seam between the two systems.
- **The LLM's role is strictly bounded to language, never computation or selection.** Repeated as an architectural rule across multiple docs (Mealvana Ai Engineering Design Document, AI Assistant Technical Architecture): algorithmic code owns macros/constraints/food-safety/matching; the LLM only renders language, and is explicitly told (via system-prompt bracket-tag protocol) that it does NOT select meals, calculate nutrition, filter, or score.
- **Recipe strategy: a component/"Lego" model, not a large recipe database.** ~15–25 structural composition patterns × ~200–300 reusable components (protein/veg/grain/sauce), each carrying its own macros/allergens/cost/substitution/cooking-method data — chosen explicitly to avoid building and maintaining a large preset recipe library. This decision directly encodes the user-research finding that most athletes eat ingredient/component-first.
- **AI assistant scope, as of 2026-06-17: exactly two validated intents** — (1) weekly meal planning + shopping list, (2) in-the-moment "what's a good snack / quick dinner" suggestions. Everything else (race fueling, recipe browsing as primary mode, deep tracking) is explicitly out of scope for the assistant.
- **The AI suggestion "sparkle" button is deliberately hidden until meal planning ships** (2026-06-25 decision) — meal planning is a hard prerequisite for good AI suggestions, since the assistant needs to know what's already planned to suggest well.
- **Meal planning belongs under a proposed new "Food" tab**, grouped with formulas, shopping list, disliked/frequently-used foods, and build-a-meal (per "Design the meal-planning page" sprint task) — not bolted onto an existing screen.
- **Meal-planning feature is a "Cookies"-tier (premium) offering** on the roadmap — explicit monetization/differentiation tier assignment versus free workout fueling.
- **Gamification: completing a weekly meal plan grants "cookies" (virtual currency)** to encourage planning-habit formation — approved in principle by Lee ("This is a good direction... we're just going to have to start off with an MVP") but flagged by Xuan as high-complexity risk ("I don't want us to jump into the deep end of it immediately") and by a reviewer (Faith Good) as gameable (users could spam fake plans to farm currency).

## 3. Open questions (unresolved as of the latest artifacts)

- **Chat vs. button-driven UI as the primary interaction model.** Never fully resolved to a single answer — research suggests segmentation (chat for AI-native heavy users, button-driven as the default with chat as an embedded option), but this was still being framed as "the most important hypothesis" as late as the Round 2 interview script, and inline page comments as recently as 2026-06-01 explicitly push back on parts of the "settled" synthesis (see Disagreements below).
- **"Log It vs. Plan It" labeling ambiguity for future-dated meals** — flagged in the scope-decision doc as a ~5-year-old, still-unfixed UX problem.
- **Macro-visibility tension.** The design-principle documents (MealBuddy prompts, technical architecture) explicitly favor a macro-number-free, training-context-language interface — but live prototype testing found users wanted visible macro numbers tied to a daily total, and confusion resulted when only per-meal numbers were shown. This contradiction is not resolved anywhere in the archive.
- **Family/household meal planning** — explicitly deferred past v1 scope, despite user research identifying it as a real adoption barrier for a meaningful share of the target population (Rebecca, Vicky, Meredith, Robin all describe family-driven constraints).
- **Recipe-database completeness/diversity** — multiple prototype-test participants flagged missing vegetarian and South-Asian recipe representation; not resolved in any product-decision doc found.
- **Ownership/ship date.** "Design the meal-planning page" sat in Backlog with no owner as of 2026-07-07; the follow-up "Work on the meal-planning design Lee asked for" task was still open and due as of 2026-08-25 — the feature had not shipped by the end of the archived record, despite being on the roadmap since at least January 2026 with an original April 2026 ship-date target.
- **Whether the shopping-list mechanism (plan → shopping list → suggest) itself is user-validated as designed**, vs. just the general underlying need — the Round 1+2 synthesis explicitly calls this "thin": the need to bridge macro targets to real food is validated, but Xuan's specific weekly-plan-tab mechanism is described as still his product vision, not fully user-tested end-to-end.
- **Disordered-eating positioning language** — the synthesis doc recommends getting Rachel Mitchell's explicit input and potentially formalizing her as a clinical advisor before launch; no evidence in the archive that this happened.

## 4. Proposed flows / features

- **Diagnose-and-add core loop** (the most validated flow concept): "tell me what you ate the last few days" → "here's what's missing for the kind of week you have ahead" → "add these specific things at these specific moments." Explicitly framed as replacing a "21-meal weekly grid" default.
- **Time-of-day / day-shape anchoring**: plans, reminders, and suggestions organized around wake time, training windows, work blocks, and family events — not breakfast/lunch/dinner category slots. Flagged as having "ripple effects through the data model."
- **Cooking-sessions grouping** for batch cooking: ~5 unique recipe cards per week (not 7 daily unique meals), each mapped to a cooking session rather than a calendar day; drag-handle re-grouping with a "Use as-is" default.
- **Card-scoped, ingredient-level swap and serving-size editing** — swap and `+/-` serving controls live directly on the meal card, not behind a separate flow; substitution recalculates macros and shopping list automatically.
- **Proactive, contextual "intelligence at the top"**: the system leads with visible awareness (training load, tapering, weather, sales) before asking the user anything — e.g., "We notice you are tapering for a race now. Next week, we're going to be hiding protein" — intended to build trust through demonstrated context rather than generic prompts.
- **Deal/inventory stitching**: MealBuddy prototype's marquee differentiator moment — surfacing a relevant grocery-store deal mid-conversation and folding it back into an already-confirmed meal, explicitly called out as the moment meant to answer "isn't this just ChatGPT?"
- **Memory drawer** ("What MealBuddy knows about you" / "What MealBuddy uses for recommendations") — a user-visible, editable transparency and control surface over the assistant's stored preferences; Variant A framed it as a trust layer, Variant B reframed it as a control layer, testing whether users read it as empowering or as surveillance.
- **Coach-side visibility, not editing** — coaches see and can suggest modifications, but per coach interviews they don't want (and don't feel qualified for) direct editing/prescription authority.
- **Nutrition Transparency** (adjacent, related feature) — a "(?)" explainability affordance anywhere the algorithm outputs a number, giving a human-readable breakdown of inputs/assumptions; directly motivated by Claudia McCoy closing the app after a recommendation felt "so off" — transparency is the proposed fix that would let a coach adjust inputs instead of abandoning the tool.
- **Shopping List feature** (existing spec, adjacent/downstream of meal planning): auto-generated, store/aisle-grouped, integrates theFeed.com's product catalog, supports coach brand-partnership prioritization, shareable; V2 explicitly slated to add cost-per-serving data to address the coach-identified cost-visibility gap.
- **Long-term / aspirational**: HelloFresh-style automatic ordering integration (suggested by coach Ryan Bolton); grocery pickup/delivery API integration (Instacart named specifically); CPG/retail-media monetization angle explored in the "AI agentic shopping Market Research" doc.

## 5. Disagreements (explicit, named, not fully resolved)

1. **Chatbot/conversational AI vs. structured browse/select vs. hybrid.** The foundational, longest-running disagreement in the whole feature. First documented explicitly in the 2026-04-07 "Feature discussion" meeting (one team member advocating AI-chatbot, another raising complexity/trust/engagement concerns and a personal preference for browsing over being told what to eat), reiterated verbatim as still unresolved in the 2026-04-06 checklist ("Two competing approaches: AI chatbot model vs. browse/select library vs. hybrid"), and crystallized by Xuan at the 2026-05-08 meeting as a **founder-level disagreement**: "there is a fundamental presumption... I think people want to be told what to eat. And Li is totally against that." This disagreement was formalized into a head-to-head research design (Variant A "suggestion-forward"/Xuan vs. Variant B "control-forward"/Lee) rather than resolved by discussion — "the team acknowledged the debate was becoming circular and agreed to... stop trying to convince each other and move forward with experimentation."
2. **Dish/recipe-level vs. ingredient-level entry** — the second axis of the same formal research design ("Testing two cofounder disagreements"), mapped onto a 2×2 competitive-quadrant framework (Suggestion×Dish = HungryRoot, Suggestion×Ingredient = Blue Apron, Control×Dish = NYT Cooking, Control×Ingredient = SuperCook).
3. **Macro-number-free design principle vs. user desire for visible macros** — not named as a disagreement between team members explicitly, but a real, documented contradiction between the prototype-design philosophy and the live prototype-testing results (see Open Questions above).
4. **Scoring-algorithm point values and validation rigor** — reviewer pushback (Lee and others, in comments on "Meal Planning Algorithm") on specific weights (e.g., arguing the recently-cooked penalty should be steeper, that the disliked-ingredient penalty is too lenient and should trigger substitution instead of just a score hit) and a broader concern that the scoring system is static and lacks any A/B-style validation or regression-test feedback loop.
5. **LLM vendor/approach churn** — the "6.19.25 LLM Meal Planning System Meeting" documents a production system built on GPT-4o Mini with explicit reasoning ("Claude is more expensive, GPT-3.5 Turbo has poor results"), while later architecture docs (mid-to-late 2026) standardize on Claude with an explicit "the LLM is not the moat" framing — a real strategic shift in vendor thinking over time, not a live disagreement but worth flagging as drift.
6. **Lee flagged unspecified disagreements with Xuan's meal-planning vision** in the 2026-06-17 scope-decision doc, explicitly deferred to a later conversation — recorded as unresolved at the time of that document.
7. **Whether the specific plan → shopping-list → suggest mechanism is "Xuan's vision" or user-validated** — the Round 1+2 synthesis is explicit that this distinction matters and that the mechanism itself is "thin" evidence versus the broader need, which is strong.

## 7. Scope

- **In scope (validated / decided):** weekly diagnose-and-add planning; auto-generated, store-grouped shopping list; component/ingredient-level swap; batch-cooking/cooking-session grouping; training-schedule-awareness (heavier days = higher-calorie plans, rest days = recovery-focused); dietary-restriction and allergy support; coach view/comment (not edit) access; in-the-moment quick-suggestion intent, gated behind meal planning shipping.
- **Explicitly out of scope / deferred:** race-day fueling logic (stays in the existing deterministic module, never LLM-routed); family/household multi-person planning (v1 targets single athletes); large curated recipe database (component/Lego model instead); daily logging as a core loop (situational/expert-only); prescriptive numeric meal plans (minimums framing instead, per disordered-eating safety positioning); HelloFresh-style automatic ordering (explicitly long-term/aspirational only).
- **"Cookies" premium tier placement** signals this is intended as a paid differentiator against the free workout-fueling core product, consistent with the Willingness-to-Pay research question framing found in the User Research index (paywalled Meal Planner in MyFitnessPal's own top tier was read as a positive willingness-to-pay signal for automated planning generally).

## 8. Tech

- **Architecture split**: algorithmic template-solver pipeline (hard filters → soft scoring → ranking → macro computation, mostly deterministic) with only two LLM-touched steps (optional constraint parsing from free text, optional flavor rerank within an already-algorithmically-selected top-N) plus a final LLM-only "render as prose" step. This is the most mature/current version of a design principle present in every architecture doc going back to the original Mealvana Ai Engineering Design Document's "LLM as interface not decision engine."
- **Conversation state machine**: 5 states (Opener → Prescription → Refinement → Confirmation → Post-plan); v1 = rule-based state machine, v2+ roadmap = LLM-guided with hard guardrails.
- **Data/offline**: Drift-local-first with `needs_upload` flag and Supabase Realtime streaming (reusing existing coach-chat infrastructure) — the LLM call itself is described as the only hard-online dependency in the flow.
- **Images**: 5-tier strategy (free stock photography → commissioned hero photography → custom illustration → user-generated content → rare, human-reviewed, cached AI-generated fallback) — explicitly designed to avoid per-request AI image generation, both for cost and for avoiding uncanny-valley artifacts, which food imagery is called out as being especially prone to.
- **Known historical failure modes** (from the 6.19.25 system-meeting doc, describing the pre-current-architecture system): LLM inconsistently calling tools or passing wrong parameters; inability to create custom meals or recommend external recipes when the recipe DB is empty; missing food-database tags (e.g., spiciness, vegan-for-all-recipes) causing filter errors. These failure modes are the direct motivation for the later architectural shift toward strict algorithmic ownership of matching/filtering and a much more constrained LLM role.
- **"Honest moat" framing** (technical architecture doc): the team explicitly argues the defensible IP is residual-budget computation (`meal_planning_budget`), household/preference personalization, the Lego-match intelligence, and deal/seasonal awareness — not the underlying LLM, which is treated as swappable ("if we swapped Claude for any other model tomorrow, this product wouldn't break").
- **Roadmap estimate**: ~16–20 weeks across 4 phases, per the current technical architecture doc.
