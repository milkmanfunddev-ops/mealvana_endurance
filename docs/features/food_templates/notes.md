## Pre-Workout Nutrition Guidelines

| Window | Carb Target | Protein | Fat | Fiber |

## During-Workout Nutrition Guidelines

### Carbohydrate Targets by Duration

| Duration | Carb Target | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| <60 min | 0-30g total | Often not needed; mouth rinse may help |
| 60-90 min | 30-60 g/hr | Start fueling at 30-45 min mark |
| 90 min-2.5 hr | 45-60 g/hr | Consistent intake every 20-30 min |
| 2.5-4 hr | 60-90 g/hr | Requires gut training for higher rates |
| 4+ hr | 80-100+ g/hr max | Only trained guts; mix glucose:fructose |

### Sport-Specific Considerations

#### Cycling
- **Max rate**: 80-120 g/hr (trained athletes)
- **Advantages**: Stable platform, easy access to bottles/pockets
- **Common foods**: Bars, gels, chews, rice cakes, sandwiches, sports drinks
- **Timing**: Every 15-20 minutes for optimal absorption

#### Running
- **Max rate**: 50-70 g/hr
- **Challenges**: GI distress from impact, blood diverted from gut
- **Common foods**: Gels, chews, sports drinks (easy to consume on the move)
- **Timing**: Every 20-45 minutes; smaller, more frequent is better

#### Swimming
- **During swim**: Generally not possible
- **Pool practice**: Quick fuel at pool deck during rest intervals
- **Open water**: Aid station stops in organized events
- **Focus**: Pre-swim and post-swim nutrition most important

### Brick Workout Adjustment
When running after cycling, reduce run nutrition by 20-30%:
- Reason: Residual fuel from bike, GI system already stressed
- Example: If targeting 60g/hr on standalone run, use 40-50g/hr post-bike

## Post-Workout Nutrition Guidelines

### Recovery Window Timing
- **0-30 minutes**: Most critical when next session <8 hours away
- **0-2 hours**: Still beneficial even with longer recovery periods
- **>24 hours until next workout**: Timing becomes flexible

### Macronutrient Targets (0-30 min)

| Nutrient | Target | Notes |
|----------|--------|-------|
| **Carbs** | 1.0-1.2 g/kg | High GI preferred for rapid glycogen resynthesis |
| **Protein** | 20-40g (0.25-0.4 g/kg) | ~3g leucine minimum for MPS |
| **Fat** | No specific target | Will slow absorption; minimize if rapid recovery needed |

### 24-Hour Recovery Totals
| Training Load | Daily Carb Target |
|---------------|-------------------|
| Moderate activity | 5-7 g/kg |
| High-volume training | 7-12 g/kg |

### Special Considerations
- **Older athletes (40+)**: May benefit from 40g protein post-workout
- **After train-low sessions**: Increase protein to 0.35-0.4 g/kg
- **High muscle damage workouts**: Consider 40g protein

## Transition Nutrition Guidelines (T1/T2)

### T1: Swim → Bike
- **Time available**: 30-60 seconds typical
- **Goals**:
  - Quick carb boost (15-30g)
  - Begin hydration
  - Optional caffeine for mental sharpness
- **Format**: Easy-open, no-chew preferred
- **Examples**: Gel taped to bike, sports drink in bottle

### T2: Bike → Run
- **Time available**: 30-60 seconds typical
- **Goals**:
  - Top off carbs (15-30g)
  - Don't overload (run = more GI stress)
  - Continue hydration
- **Format**: Quick consume, easy on stomach
- **Examples**: Half gel, few swigs sports drink, gel flask

### Key Principles for Transitions
1. **Pre-stage everything**: No fumbling with packaging
2. **Practice in training**: Never try new foods on race day
3. **Less is more**: Better to under-fuel briefly than cause GI distress
4. **Liquid > solid**: Faster absorption, less chewing

## Template Validation Process

1. Calculate base serving macros
2. Test scaling against all 4 personas
3. Verify carb targets hit within ±10% of minimum
4. Check protein/fat limits for timing window
5. Document scaling notes for each persona
6. Mark validation status
