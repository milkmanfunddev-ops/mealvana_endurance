# During-Bike Fueling Research for Cyclists and Triathletes

**Research Date:** January 21, 2026
**Purpose:** Comprehensive research on cycling nutrition strategies for road cycling, Ironman bike legs, and century rides

---

## Executive Summary

Cycling nutrition has undergone a **"carbohydrate revolution"** in recent years, with recommendations increasing from 30-60g/hr to 60-90g/hr for recreational athletes and up to 120-180g/hr for elite athletes. The stable platform of cycling allows for more solid food consumption compared to running, though gut training is essential for high carb intake rates.

**Key Findings:**
- **Standard Target:** 60-90g carbs/hour for rides over 2.5 hours
- **Trained Athletes:** 80-120g carbs/hour with proper gut training
- **Elite Athletes:** 120-180g carbs/hour (Tour de France, Ironman pros)
- **Hydration:** 16-32oz fluid/hour with 1,000mg sodium/hour
- **Food Variety:** Solid foods (rice cakes, PB&J) popular alongside gels/drinks
- **Gut Training:** 6-10 week progressive protocol to build tolerance

## Current Carbohydrate Guidelines (2025-2026)

### By Ride Duration

| Duration | Carb Target | Rationale |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| < 60 min | Not needed | Glycogen stores sufficient |
| 60-90 min | 30-60g/hr | Optional for high intensity |
| 2-2.5 hrs | 60g/hr | Prevent bonking |
| > 2.5 hrs | 60-90g/hr | Sustained energy |
| > 3 hrs (trained) | 80-120g/hr | With gut training |
| Elite racing | 120-180g/hr | Requires months of preparation |

### By Intensity

- **Zone 1-2 (Recovery/Endurance):** 30-60g/hr
- **Zone 3 (Tempo):** 60-80g/hr
- **Zone 4-5 (Threshold/VO2):** 80-120g/hr

### Multi-Transportable Carbohydrates

**CRITICAL:** To absorb >60g/hr, use multiple carbohydrate types that utilize different intestinal transporters:

- **Glucose + Fructose:** 2:1 or 1:0.8 ratio optimal
- **Example Products:** Maurten, SIS Beta Fuel, Tailwind
- **Mechanism:** Glucose uses SGLT1 transporter, fructose uses GLUT5
- **Result:** Up to 90-120g/hr absorption vs 60g/hr single-source limit

## Sport-Specific Guidelines

### Century Rides (100 Miles / 160km)

**Target:** 60g carbs/hr (100-250 calories/hr)
**Duration:** 4-7 hours
**Strategy:**
- Start fueling 30-60 minutes into ride
- Eat and drink early and often (every 15-20 min)
- Mix solid foods with gels/drinks to avoid flavor fatigue
- Monitor fluid intake: 26-28oz/hr maximum

**Pre-Ride:**
- Carbohydrate-rich meal 2-3 hours before (oatmeal + banana, rice + chicken)
- Taper activity while maintaining 60-65% carb diet the week before

### Ironman Bike Leg (112 Miles / 180km)

**Target:** 90g carbs/hr (recreational), 120-180g/hr (elite)
**Duration:** 4.5-7 hours
**Strategy:**
- Begin feeding immediately after swim exit
- Set watch reminder every 15-20 minutes
- Consume 1,000mg sodium/hr
- Bank higher carbs on bike (90g/hr) to enable lower intake on run (60g/hr)

**Elite Examples (2025 Ironman World Championships):**
- **Bike:** 145g carbs/hr, 41oz fluid/hr, 1,103mg sodium/hr
- **Run:** Scale back to 60g/hr in hot conditions
- **Requirement:** 6+ months of gut training

### Tour de France / Pro Racing

**Target:** 120g carbs/hr (flat stages: 60g/hr, mountains: 120g/hr)
**Strategy:**
- Pre-race: 3.5g carbs per kg body weight 4 hours before
- During: Consistent 60-120g/hr depending on terrain
- Recovery: 2-2.5g protein per kg body weight post-stage
- Energy expenditure: Up to 10,000 calories/day

## Gut Training Protocol (6-10 Week Timeline)

### Phase 1: Baseline (Weeks 1-2)
**Target:** Current tolerance + 10g/hr
**Focus:** Establish baseline, add small increment
**Example:** If tolerating 40g/hr → increase to 50g/hr

### Phase 2: Volume Tolerance (Weeks 3-4)
**Target:** 60-70g/hr
**Focus:** Train stomach to handle volume of food/liquid
**Strategy:** Mix of solids and liquids, practice eating every 15-20 min

### Phase 3: Multiple Transportable Carbs (Weeks 5-6)
**Target:** 70-90g/hr
**Focus:** Add fructose sources, train digestive pathways
**Products:** Switch to 2:1 glucose:fructose formulas
**Timeline:** Transporter density changes measurable in 1-2 weeks

### Phase 4: Race Intensity Practice (Weeks 7-8)
**Target:** 90-100g/hr at race pace
**Focus:** Practice at Zone 3-4 intensity while fueling
**Key:** Do this in final key training rides, slightly above race target

### Phase 5: Fine-Tuning (Weeks 9-10)
**Target:** Race-day target (90-120g/hr depending on goal)
**Focus:** Nail exact product mix, timing, flavors
**Practice:** Dress rehearsal rides with exact race nutrition

### Advanced: Elite Protocol (120g/hr+)
**Timeline:** 3-6 months progressive training
**Requirements:**
- Months of consistent gut training
- Professional nutritionist guidance
- Metabolic testing
- Practice at race-day intensities

**Key Adaptations:**
- Gastric emptying improves: 3-7 days
- Transporter density changes: 1-2 weeks
- Full tolerance to 120g/hr: 4-6 months

## Key Insights from Research

### 1. The Carbohydrate Revolution
Athletes are consuming 2-3x more carbs than a decade ago. What was once considered "too much" (90g/hr) is now standard for competitive athletes, with elites pushing 120-180g/hr.

### 2. Solid Foods Are Back
Despite the gel/drink dominance, pros still rely heavily on rice cakes, sandwiches, and savory foods for long rides. The stable cycling platform allows this, unlike running.

### 3. Flavor Fatigue Is Real
Around hours 3-4, sweet flavors become unappealing. Savory options (bacon rice cakes, pretzels, potato) provide psychological relief and continued fueling.

### 4. Sodium Matters More Than We Thought
Current recommendations: 1,000mg/hr for long efforts. Tailwind leads with 606mg per 200 calories. Many athletes add salt capsules.

### 5. Practice Makes Perfect
**"Fuel that sits well in Zone 1-2 training might not sit well at Zone 3 racing."** All nutrition must be practiced at race intensity and in race conditions (heat, humidity).

### 6. Individual Variation
Some athletes can't tolerate gels and fuel exclusively on solid foods. Others prefer all-liquid. There's no one-size-fits-all approach.

### 7. The 15-20 Minute Rule
Set a watch reminder. Consistent small doses beat infrequent large doses. Prevention is easier than recovery from bonking.

## Recommendations for App Implementation

### 1. Tiered Carb Targets Based on Training Level

**Beginner (No Gut Training):**
- 60g/hr for rides >2.5 hours
- Focus on simple, familiar foods
- Templates: #1, #2, #7, #11

**Intermediate (Some Gut Training):**
- 70-90g/hr for rides >2.5 hours
- Can handle sport-specific products
- Templates: #3, #4, #5, #8, #12

**Advanced (6+ Weeks Gut Training):**
- 90-120g/hr for racing
- Multiple transportable carbs required
- Templates: #6, #10

### 2. Food Category Distinctions

**Cycling-Friendly Solids:** (Unlike running, these work well on bike)
- Rice cakes
- Sandwiches (PB&J, roll-ups)
- Waffles/stroopwafels
- Bars (not too chewy)
- Boiled potatoes

**Gels:** (Same as running, but less exclusively used)
- Maurten, GU, SIS, etc.

**Drink Mixes:** (Higher carb concentrations than running)
- 80g/serving options (Maurten 320, SIS Beta Fuel)
- 50g/serving options (Tailwind)
- Regular sports drinks (20-25g)

**Savory Options:** (Unique to cycling)
- Pretzels
- Potato chips
- Bacon rice cakes
- Salted nuts

### 3. Gut Training Guidance

Add educational content:
- "Want to fuel like a pro? Start a 6-10 week gut training program"
- Progressive targets: 60g → 70g → 80g → 90g
- Emphasize practice at race intensity

### 4. Ironman-Specific Considerations

- **Bike leg:** Higher carb target (90g/hr)
- **Run leg:** Lower carb target (60g/hr) - reference existing running templates
- **Strategy:** "Bank" carbs on bike to enable lower run fueling

### 5. Sodium Tracking

- Target: 1,000mg/hr
- Flag low-sodium plans
- Suggest adding salt capsules when needed
