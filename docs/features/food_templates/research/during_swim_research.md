# During-Swim Fueling Research

**Research Date**: January 21, 2026
**Purpose**: Determine if during-swim nutrition templates are needed for swimmers and triathletes

---

## Executive Summary

**Key Finding**: During-swim fueling is context-dependent and varies dramatically by scenario:

1. **Pool Practice (<1 hour)**: No fueling needed
2. **Pool Practice (>1 hour)**: Sports drinks and quick snacks between sets
3. **Ironman/Triathlon Swim Leg**: No fueling possible during swim (pre-race only)
4. **Open Water Races (<5km)**: Generally no fueling needed
5. **Open Water Races (>10km/marathon)**: Feed stations with kayak support every 15-30 minutes

**Recommendation**: **YES, include during-swim templates** - but only for two specific scenarios:
- Pool practice breaks (>1 hour workouts)
- Marathon/ultra-distance open water racing (>10km with feed station support)

Standard triathlon swims (even Ironman distance at 2.4 miles) do NOT require during-swim fueling.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Pool Practice Fueling

**When it's needed**:
- Workouts longer than 1 hour require fueling during practice
- Swimmers lose significant water through sweat despite being in water
- Coaches should build fueling breaks into workouts between sets

**What swimmers consume**:
- Primary: Sports drinks (water + electrolytes + carbs)
- Target: 16-24 oz water per 1-hour workout
- Quick snacks between sets for energy boosts

**Sources**:
- [What Swimmers Should Eat Before, During and After Swimming - MySwimPro](https://blog.myswimpro.com/2020/11/03/what-swimmers-should-eat-before-during-and-after-swimming/)
- [Fueling for Success: What and When to Eat For Optimal Performance - Swimming World](https://www.swimmingworldmagazine.com/news/fueling-for-success-what-and-when-to-eat-for-optimal-performance/)

### 2. Triathlon Swim Legs

**Critical finding**: You CANNOT eat during triathlon swims, including Ironman distance.

**Why**:
- Swimming mechanics make eating impossible while maintaining pace
- Even the 2.4-mile Ironman swim takes only 60-90 minutes for most athletes
- Athletes rely entirely on pre-race nutrition

**Strategy**:
- Focus on carb-heavy dinner night before
- Light carb-rich breakfast 2-3 hours before start
- Optional energy gel immediately before swim start (practiced in training)
- Begin fueling immediately upon exiting swim onto bike leg

**Sources**:
- [Ironman Nutrition Guide for Swim, Bike, and Run – Map Medal](https://mapmedal.com/blogs/news/ironman-nutrition)
- [How to fuel the Ironman | 220 Triathlon](https://www.220triathlon.com/training/nutrition-training/how-to-fuel-the-ironman)

### 3. Open Water Racing

**Distance thresholds**:
- **<5km**: Pre-race glycogen loading sufficient; no during-race fueling needed
- **5-10km**: May need minimal fueling if duration >60-90 minutes
- **>10km**: Requires consistent feeding every 15-30 minutes

**Feeding infrastructure**:
- Fixed/floating pontoons for shorter races
- Personal kayak support for point-to-point and marathon swims
- Most lap-based races prohibit individual kayak support

**Carbohydrate targets**:
- 60-90g carbs per hour for swims >10km
- Up to 90g/hr from multitransportable carbohydrate sources

**Sources**:
- [How to Fuel Your Open Water Swim | U.S. Masters Swimming](https://www.usms.org/fitness-and-training/articles-and-videos/articles/how-to-fuel-your-open-water-swim?Oldid=3370)
- [The Best Open Water Swimming Nutrition Guide](https://eatswimwin.com/the-best-open-water-swimming-nutrtion-guide/)
- [Feeding station - Openwaterpedia](https://www.openwaterpedia.com/wiki/Feeding_station)

### 4. Marathon/Ultra-Distance Swimming

**Feeding frequency**:
- Common intervals: Every 15-30 minutes
- Many swimmers feed hourly for first 2-3 hours, then switch to shorter intervals
- Speed is critical: Fastest swimmers spend <60 seconds feeding per hour

**Nutrition content**:
- Primary fuel: Warm carbohydrate drinks (Maxim/maltodextrin most popular)
- 200-300ml sports drink providing 15-25g carbs + electrolytes
- Energy gels (especially those not requiring water)
- For races >3 hours: Add protein to carbohydrates

**Temperature considerations**:
- Cold water (15°C): 800-900 calories/hour needed (vs 600-700 in warm water)
- Warm or room-temperature drinks preferred in cold water
- Cold demands extra energy for thermoregulation

**Feeding methods**:
- Kayak feeding (fastest method, requires training with support crew)
- Feed stations/pontoons
- Tow floats with storage
- Soft flasks in wetsuit

**Sources**:
- [Feeding the Marathon Swimmer – Catalina Channel Swimming Federation](https://swimcatalina.org/feeding-the-marathon-swimmer/)
- [What should I eat during a marathon swim? - Outdoor Swimmer Magazine](https://outdoorswimmer.com/coach/what-should-i-eat-during-a-marathon-swim/)
- [How To: Understand Important Factors in Marathon Swim Feeding | LoneSwimmer](https://loneswimmer.com/2012/03/05/howto-important-factors-in-marathon-swim-feeding/)

## Scenarios Identified

### Scenario 1: Pool Practice Breaks (>1 hour workouts)

**Context**:
- Competitive swimmers or triathletes doing long pool training sessions
- Built-in rest breaks between sets (wall breaks, coaching feedback)
- Need quick, non-perishable foods that won't cause GI distress

**Timing**: Between sets during 60-90+ minute workouts

**Constraints**:
- Must consume quickly (30-60 seconds)
- Must be easy to digest (low fat, high carb)
- Must be non-perishable (kept poolside)
- Must not weigh swimmer down

### Scenario 2: Open Water Marathon/Ultra-Distance Racing (>10km)

**Context**:
- Events with feed station or kayak support infrastructure
- Swims lasting multiple hours (often 3-12+ hours)
- Cold water exposure requiring extra calories

**Timing**: Every 15-30 minutes

**Constraints**:
- Must consume while treading water (minimize time stopped)
- Must work with kayak feeding or feed station delivery
- Must provide 60-90g carbs per hour
- Temperature of drink matters (warm for cold water)
- Must be practiced extensively in training

## Proposed During-Swim Templates

### Template Category 1: Pool Practice Breaks (Quick Fuel Between Sets)

#### Template 1.1: "Minimal Hydration Break"
**Target**: 60-75 minute practices
- 8-12 oz sports drink (e.g., Gatorade, Nuun)
- Total carbs: ~15-20g
- **Notes**: Consumed at wall between sets; no solid food needed

#### Template 1.2: "Quick Energy Boost"
**Target**: 90-120 minute practices
- 16 oz sports drink
- ½ banana OR 1-2 dates
- Total carbs: ~40-50g
- **Notes**: One break midway through practice; banana can be kept in bag

#### Template 1.3: "Extended Practice Fuel"
**Target**: 2+ hour morning practices
- 20-24 oz sports drink
- Small handful (1 oz) dried fruit (raisins, banana chips)
- 10-15 pretzels
- Total carbs: ~60-70g
- **Notes**: Two breaks (hourly); all items non-perishable and poolside-friendly

#### Template 1.4: "Double-Session Fuel"
**Target**: Between two practices same day
- 12-16 oz sports drink
- 1 applesauce pouch
- Small handful Goldfish crackers
- Total carbs: ~45-55g
- **Notes**: Consumed during 30-60 minute break between sessions

---

### Template Category 2: Marathon/Ultra Open Water (Feed Station/Kayak Support)

#### Template 2.1: "Standard Marathon Feed (Warm Water)"
**Target**: 10km-25km swims, water >18°C, hourly feeds
- 8-10 oz warm carb drink (Maxim/maltodextrin, 6-8% concentration)
- 1 energy gel
- Total carbs: ~60-70g per feed
- **Notes**: Feed every 30-60 minutes; drink should be body temperature; practice in training

#### Template 2.2: "Cold Water Marathon Feed"
**Target**: Channel swims, water <16°C, frequent feeds
- 10-12 oz warm carb drink (8-10% concentration)
- 1-2 energy gels (one with caffeine after first 2 hours)
- Optional: ¼ avocado or spoonful peanut butter
- Total carbs: ~80-100g per feed
- **Notes**: Feed every 20-30 minutes; warm liquid critical for thermoregulation

#### Template 2.3: "Ultra-Distance Real Food Feed (>6 hours)"
**Target**: 25km+ swims, ultra-marathon events
- 8 oz warm carb drink
- 1 energy gel
- 1 "treat" (mini flapjack, brioche, rice pudding pot, or Battenburg cake slice)
- Total carbs: ~90-110g per feed
- **Notes**: Alternate between gel-only feeds and real food feeds; real food for mental boost

#### Template 2.4: "Minimal Marathon Feed (Experienced Athletes)"
**Target**: Fast swimmers minimizing feed time
- 6 oz concentrated carb drink (10-12% solution)
- 1 gel (no water needed type)
- Total carbs: ~70-80g
- **Notes**: <30 second feed stop; concentrate solution to reduce volume; only for athletes with high gut tolerance

#### Template 2.5: "Protein-Enhanced Ultra Feed (>3 hours)"
**Target**: Very long swims requiring protein
- 8 oz warm carb drink
- 1 energy gel
- 1 peanut butter packet OR premade protein shake (2-3 oz)
- Total carbs: ~70-80g + ~8-10g protein per feed
- **Notes**: Start protein feeds after first 3 hours; helps prevent muscle breakdown

## Recommendation

### Include During-Swim Templates: **YES**

**Rationale**:

1. **Two distinct use cases exist**:
   - Pool practice fueling is common among competitive swimmers and triathletes training >1 hour
   - Marathon open water racing is a growing segment requiring specialized nutrition knowledge

2. **Not applicable to standard triathlons**:
   - Ironman/Olympic distance triathlon swims do NOT require during-swim fueling
   - This should be clearly communicated to users to avoid confusion

3. **User education needed**:
   - Most endurance athletes will never need during-swim templates
   - Templates should include clear guidance on when they apply
   - App should ask qualifying questions (e.g., "Is this a pool practice >1 hour?" or "Is this a marathon swim >10km?")

4. **Differentiation from pre-swim**:
   - Pre-swim templates handle all triathlon scenarios
   - During-swim templates are specialized for pool practice breaks and ultra-distance racing only

## Implementation Notes

### User Interface Considerations

**Template Selection Flow**:
```
User selects "Swimming" activity
  → "When will you eat?"
    → "Before swim" (default for most users)
    → "During swim"
      → "What type of swim?"
        → "Pool practice break (>1 hour)" → Show Templates 1.1-1.4
        → "Marathon/ultra-distance race (>10km with feed support)" → Show Templates 2.1-2.5
```

**Warning Text for "During Swim" Option**:
> "Most swimmers, including those doing Ironman triathlons, do NOT need during-swim nutrition. Select this only if you're doing extended pool practices (>1 hour) or marathon open water races (>10km) with feed station support."

## Key Takeaways for Development Team

1. **During-swim templates ARE valuable** but serve a niche audience (competitive pool swimmers and ultra-distance racers)

2. **Clear user guidance is critical** to prevent confusion - most users won't need these templates

3. **Two distinct template categories** with different food requirements and feeding strategies

4. **Practice-focused language** - emphasize that all feeding strategies must be tested in training, never tried for first time in competition

5. **Temperature matters** for marathon swimming - warm drinks are essential in cold water for both performance and safety

6. **Speed is critical** for marathon feeds - fastest swimmers minimize feeding time to <60 seconds per hour

7. **Individual variation is high** - templates should be starting points, with strong encouragement to experiment in training
