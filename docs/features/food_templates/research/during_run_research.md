# During-Run Fueling Research for Endurance Runners
**Research Date:** January 21, 2026
**Purpose:** Identify popular during-run fueling templates for marathon, ultra, and trail runners

---

## Executive Summary: Guidelines & Carbohydrate Targets

### Carbohydrate Intake by Duration

| Run Duration | Carbs/Hour Target | Notes |
|-------------|-------------------|-------|
| 60-90 minutes | 30-40g/hr | Light fueling, can use gels or chews |
| 90 minutes - 2.5 hours | 30-60g/hr | Most marathoners fall here; 50g/hr is good starting point |
| 2.5-3 hours (marathon) | 60-90g/hr | Research shows 60g/hr = ~11 min faster finish time |
| 3+ hours (ultra) | 60-90g/hr | Up to 120g/hr for elite, but requires gut training |

### Key Principles from Research

1. **Start Early:** Begin fueling at 15-30 minutes into the run, not when you feel depleted
2. **Frequency:** Take nutrition every 30-45 minutes (or every 5-6km in races)
3. **Gut Training Required:** High carb intake (90g+/hr) requires practice during training
4. **Hydration:** 0.4-0.8L/hr (400-800ml/hr) with 300-600mg sodium/hr
5. **Never Try Anything New on Race Day:** All nutrition must be tested in training
6. **Variety for Ultras:** Alternate sweet/savory, gels/chews/real food to prevent flavor fatigue

### Research-Backed Performance Impact

- Runners consuming 60g carbs/hr completed marathons ~11 minutes faster than those who didn't fuel
- 160 marathon runners at Seville Marathon: those consuming 60-90g/hr were more likely to finish sub-3 hours
- Gut capacity can be trained - runners who practice high carb intake don't experience more GI distress in races

## 10+ Popular During-Run Fueling Templates

### Template 1: Classic Marathon Gel Schedule
**Best For:** First-time marathoners, 3:30-5:00 finish times

- **Foods:**
  - 6 x GU Energy Gels (100 cal, 22-25g carbs each)
  - Water at aid stations
- **Carbs/Hour:** ~50-60g
- **Schedule:**
  - Mile 6: First gel
  - Then every 4-5 miles (every 40-50 min)
  - Total: 6 gels over 26.2 miles
- **Notes:** Most common beginner approach; simple to execute; GU has 20+ flavors to prevent palate fatigue

**Specific Product Example:**
- Vanilla Bean, Chocolate Outrage, Salted Caramel (variety for palatability)

---

### Template 2: Maurten High-Carb Marathon Plan
**Best For:** Sub-3 hour marathoners, elite runners

- **Foods:**
  - 7 x Maurten Gel 100 (25g carbs each)
  - Optional: 2 x Maurten Gel 100 CAF (with 100mg caffeine)
- **Carbs/Hour:** 60-70g
- **Schedule:**
  - Start at 15-20 minutes
  - 1 gel every 6km (~25-30 min intervals)
  - Caffeine gels at miles 18-20 for late-race boost
- **Notes:** Hydrogel technology for easier digestion; neutral taste; premium price ($3-4/gel); used by many pro marathoners

**Pre-Race Addition:**
- Maurten Drink Mix 320 (80g carbs) 2-3 hours before start

---

### Template 3: Gel + Sports Drink Combo
**Best For:** Runners who prefer liquid calories, hot weather races

- **Foods:**
  - 3-4 x Energy gels (any brand)
  - Sports drink at every aid station (14g carbs per 8oz cup)
- **Carbs/Hour:** 50-70g
- **Schedule:**
  - Gel at miles 6, 12, 18, 22
  - Sports drink at every aid station (every 2-3 miles)
- **Notes:** Addresses hydration + fuel simultaneously; lighter stomach feel than gel-only; must know aid station locations

**Hydration Target:**
- 4-6oz every 15-20 minutes = ~16-24oz/hour

---

### Template 4: SIS Isotonic No-Water-Needed
**Best For:** Runners who hate carrying water, prefer minimal gear

- **Foods:**
  - 6-8 x SIS GO Isotonic Gels (22g carbs each)
- **Carbs/Hour:** 55-65g
- **Schedule:**
  - Every 30-35 minutes
- **Notes:** Can be taken WITHOUT water (isotonic formula); pleasant fruit flavors; larger packets than competitors; slightly lower carbs per gel

**Trade-off:**
- Bulkier packaging vs convenience of no water needed

---

### Template 5: Energy Chews Strategy
**Best For:** Runners who dislike gel texture, prefer chewing

- **Foods:**
  - 6-8 packets energy chews (24g carbs per pack, typically 6 chews)
  - Water at aid stations
- **Carbs/Hour:** 48-64g
- **Schedule:**
  - 1 packet every 35-45 minutes
  - Can eat 2-3 chews at a time for gradual intake
- **Notes:** Easier to dose gradually; less stomach sloshing; requires chewing (harder during heavy breathing)

**Popular Brands:**
- GU Energy Chews, Clif Shot Bloks, Honey Stinger Energy Chews

---

### Template 6: Natural/Real Food Ultra Strategy
**Best For:** Ultra marathons 4+ hours, runners with gel fatigue

- **Foods:**
  - 2-3 x Energy gels (early race)
  - 2-3 x Dates or dried fruit (100 cal, 18g carbs per 2 dates)
  - Pretzels (salted, 23g carbs per oz)
  - Boiled potatoes with salt (aid station food)
  - Coca-Cola or ginger ale (late race)
- **Carbs/Hour:** 60-80g
- **Schedule:**
  - First 2 hours: Gels every 45 min
  - Hours 2-4: Alternate dates, pretzels, waffles
  - Hours 4+: Whatever sounds good (real food, soda)
- **Notes:** Variety prevents flavor fatigue; savory options offset sweet gel overload; requires aid station support or pack

---

### Template 7: Huma Chia-Based Natural Plan
**Best For:** Runners preferring whole food ingredients, sensitive stomachs

- **Foods:**
  - 6-8 x Huma Gels (fruit puree + chia seeds, 21-25g carbs)
- **Carbs/Hour:** 42-60g
- **Schedule:**
  - Every 30-40 minutes
- **Notes:** All-natural ingredients (fruit puree, cane sugar, chia); slower absorption due to fiber; easier on stomach; premium price; unique texture (not as thick as traditional gels)

**Caution:**
- Higher fiber content - practice in training to ensure no GI issues

---

### Template 8: Caffeine-Boosted Late Race Strategy
**Best For:** Experienced marathoners, those who respond well to caffeine

- **Foods:**
  - Miles 1-16: 4 x Regular gels (non-caffeinated)
  - Miles 18-24: 3 x Caffeinated gels (50-100mg caffeine each)
- **Carbs/Hour:** 50-60g
- **Schedule:**
  - Regular gels: Miles 6, 10, 14, 16
  - Caffeine gels: Miles 18, 20, 22
- **Notes:** Caffeine delay prevents early jitters; 1-3mg/kg bodyweight is optimal dose; ~200mg total for 150lb runner

**Timing Rationale:**
- Caffeine peaks 30-60 min after intake
- Miles 18-22 is when most runners hit "the wall"

---

### Template 9: Spring Energy Whole Food Long Run
**Best For:** Trail runners, ultras, runners avoiding processed foods

- **Foods:**
  - Spring Energy gels (made from real food: sweet potato, maple syrup, etc.)
  - Dates or fig bars
  - Salted nuts (small amounts)
- **Carbs/Hour:** 50-70g
- **Schedule:**
  - Gel every 45 minutes
  - Alternate with real food every 90 minutes
- **Notes:** Whole food ingredients; more natural energy; higher price point; some products include fat/protein for sustained energy

---

### Template 10: High-Volume Carb Loading (90-120g/hr)
**Best For:** Elite runners, those with trained guts, ultras

- **Foods:**
  - Maurten Gel 160 or dual-source gels (40g carbs per gel)
  - Sports drink with 2:1 glucose:fructose ratio
- **Carbs/Hour:** 90-120g
- **Schedule:**
  - Gel every 20-30 minutes
  - Sports drink continuously (small sips)
- **Notes:** REQUIRES extensive gut training (months of practice); dual-source carbs (glucose + fructose) allow higher absorption; research shows performance gains but NOT for untrained guts

**Warning:**
- Do NOT attempt without 8-12 weeks of progressive carb training

---

### Template 11: Trail Running Mixed-Source Strategy
**Best For:** Trail ultras with varied terrain and aid station access

- **Foods:**
  - 3-4 x Gels (early miles, steep climbs)
  - Energy waffles/stroopwafels (21g carbs, mid-race)
  - Pretzels, crackers, chips (aid stations)
  - Watermelon, oranges (aid stations)
  - Broth or soup (cold weather/night running)
- **Carbs/Hour:** 50-70g
- **Schedule:**
  - Rolling/runnable terrain: Gels every 45 min
  - Steep climbs: Chewable foods (easier to consume while power hiking)
  - Aid stations: Top off with real food + electrolytes
- **Notes:** Terrain dictates format; slower pace allows real food digestion; prioritize variety to prevent nausea

---

### Template 12: Budget-Friendly Marathon Plan
**Best For:** Cost-conscious runners, training runs

- **Foods:**
  - Homemade energy chews (dates blended with honey + salt)
  - Generic sports drink powder
  - Fig bars or energy bars (cut into quarters)
- **Carbs/Hour:** 50-60g
- **Schedule:**
  - 2-3 dates or homemade chews every 40 minutes
  - Diluted sports drink in handheld bottle
- **Notes:** $5-10 total vs $25-40 for branded products; requires prep time; great for training; test before racing

## Key Insights for Template Implementation

### What Makes a Good Template

1. **Simplicity:** Easy to remember and execute during race stress
2. **Tested:** Must be practiced multiple times in training
3. **Variety:** For long events (3+ hours), alternate flavors/types
4. **Timing:** Consistent intervals (every 30-45 min) beats "when I feel hungry"
5. **Hydration Integration:** Template must specify water/electrolyte intake
6. **Backup Options:** Elite runners carry 1-2 extra gels "just in case"

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Starting too late (waiting until mile 10+ for first gel)
2. Taking all gels at once vs spreading throughout race
3. Not practicing exact race-day nutrition in training
4. Forgetting electrolytes (sodium, potassium)
5. Choosing only sweet flavors (causes palate fatigue in ultras)
6. Under-fueling due to GI fear (untrained gut can learn)

### Template Customization Factors

- **Body Weight:** Heavier runners may need slightly more carbs
- **Pace:** Faster = higher carb oxidation = more fuel needed
- **Temperature:** Hot weather = prioritize hydration with electrolytes
- **Gut Sensitivity:** Some runners need lower fiber, isotonic, or real food
- **Race Length:** Longer races require variety to prevent flavor fatigue
- **Training Volume:** Higher mileage runners often have better fat adaptation and can fuel slightly less
